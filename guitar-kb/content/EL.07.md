### The melody is the best lead part
In worship, the strongest instrumental line is often just **the song's melody**, played simply. Hymns are perfect practice: public domain, and you already know them.

### Amazing Grace
{{song:SONG_amazing_grace}}

The whole melody uses only the **G major pentatonic** (EL.01). The first phrase, in scale degrees:

| Lyric | A- | ma- | zing | grace | how | sweet | the | sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Degree | 5 | 1 | 3 1 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 6 | 5 |
| Note in G | D | G | B G | B | A | G | E | D |

### Find the rest by ear
1. Sing the next phrase, then hunt for each note in the G box.
2. When you find it, play it as **octaves** (EL.04): bigger sound, easy to follow.
3. Add a **slide** into the long notes (EL.03) and **vibrato** on the last note of each line (EL.05).

> Play the melody slightly *behind* the singer in your head, like a violin answering a vocal. That's the feel of a good worship lead.

### Goal
The melody, with the right notes and in time.
