### Delay as a rhythm partner
With a **dotted-eighth delay** (T.07, ER.03), a simple melody played in eighths turns into a cascading pattern. It's the sound of countless worship intros.

### Set up
- Delay time 60000 ÷ BPM × 0.75 (at 100 BPM: 450 ms), 2–4 repeats, mix just under the dry signal.
- Clean or light drive. Long reverb behind it.

### Play
1. Choose 3–4 notes from the G major pentatonic, high on strings 2 and 1.
2. Pick **straight eighths**, cycling through them: e.g. B D E D, B D E D.
3. The repeats land between your notes. If it sounds messy, the delay time is wrong or your eighths aren't even.
4. Change one note on each chord change (G, D, Em, C) so the pattern follows the harmony.

The graded run is 8 bars of this: one note on every eighth, no rests.

> Keep the picking hand light and steady. The delay amplifies any unevenness.

### Goal
8 bars of eighth notes at 100 BPM, average error ≤ 30 ms.
