### Why strings 6 and 5
Every barre chord and power chord takes its name from the note under your index finger on string 6 or string 5. Know those two strings and you can find any chord.

### The natural notes
The musical alphabet is **A B C D E F G**, then it repeats. Between most neighbors there's one fret gap (a sharp or flat), except **B–C** and **E–F**, which are right next to each other.

| Fret | 0 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| String 6 | E | F | G | A | B | C | D | E |
| String 5 | A | A#/Bb | C | D | E | F | G | A |

### Sharps and flats
The fret between two letters has two names: one fret above G is **G#** ("G sharp"), and it's also one fret below A, **Ab** ("A flat"). Same note, same fret.

### Landmarks
- Fret **12** repeats the open string, an octave up.
- Use the dot markers: on string 6 the dots at 3, 5, 7 are **G, A, B**. On string 5 they're **C, D, E**.
- From any landmark, count up or down one fret per half step.

> Practice idea: say the note names out loud while you play each fret on string 6 from 0 to 12. Then string 5.
