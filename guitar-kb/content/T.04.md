### What a capo does
A capo is a movable nut. Every fret it moves up raises everything by a half step, while your fingers keep playing the same **shapes**. That's how worship guitarists play in Ab, B or Eb with easy G, C, D, E and A shapes.

### The two formulas
- **Sounding key = shape key + capo fret.** G shapes with capo 2 sound in A.
- **Capo fret = song key − shape key** (count up the half steps). To play in Bb with G shapes: G → G# → A → Bb is 3 half steps, so **capo 3**.

| Song key | Good options |
|---|---|
| A | capo 2 · G shapes |
| Bb | capo 3 · G shapes, or capo 1 · A shapes |
| B | capo 4 · G shapes, or capo 2 · A shapes |
| C | open C, or capo 5 · G shapes |
| Db | capo 1 · C shapes |
| Eb | capo 1 · D shapes, or capo 3 · C shapes |
| F | capo 1 · E shapes, or capo 3 · D shapes |

> Below capo 5 or so, G shapes sound full. Higher capos sound bright and thin: great for a second acoustic, not for the only guitar.
