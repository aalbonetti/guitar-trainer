# Worship Guitar Craft

Original content. This covers the playing skills that are specific to worship, as opposed to general guitar skills.

## Roles in the band
| Instrument | Main job | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Acoustic | Rhythm and time, chord foundation, often leads the song in small settings | Overplaying in sparse sections, strumming against the drummer's feel |
| Electric (rhythm) | Texture, drive, and the dynamic lift in choruses and bridges | Doubling the acoustic's open chords, piling on low-end mud |
| Electric (lead) | Hooks, melody lines in intros and turnarounds, ambient color | Playing over the vocals, constant soloing |
**Rule of thumb:** the electric plays *higher* and *fewer notes* than the acoustic, using top-string triads, octaves, and pulses.

## Song dynamics map (typical arrangement)
| Section | Energy | Acoustic | Electric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 2–4/10 | Arpeggios or sparse strums | Hook or melody in octaves, or swells |
| Verse 1 | 2–3 | Whole-note or half-note strums | Swells, pads, or nothing |
| Pre-chorus | 4–5 | Eighth notes, building | Root pulse with dotted-eighth delay |
| Chorus | 7–8 | Full worship pattern | Triads or driving open voicings, light overdrive |
| Verse 2 | 4–5 | Eighth-note strum | Sparse triads or pulse |
| Bridge | Build from 3 → 9 | Start sparse, then build to sixteenths | Pulse → full barre chords or power chords, more drive |
| Final chorus | 9–10 | Full | Biggest part: octave hook or full chords |
| Tag / outro | Drop to 3 | Single strums, ring out | Swells, harmonics, hook reprise |

## Playing with a click & in-ears
- Count-ins are usually 1 or 2 bars. Watch the leader for cues: "verse 2," "bridge," "all in," "drop."
- On acoustic, lock your strumming to the hi-hat. On electric, lock the pulse to the kick drum and the delay to the click.
- Have a plan for recovering if you lose your place: play a single note on the downbeat until you're back.

## Charts: Nashville Number System
Worship teams usually use number charts so they can change keys quickly. In the key of G, "1 5 6m 4" means G D Em C. A chord with a dash under it, or a diamond, means hold it. A "push" mark (^) means anticipate the beat. The app should teach reading numbers as well as letter names. See T.03.

## Capo practice
Most worship acoustic playing uses **G-, C-, D-, E-, and A-shape** chords with a capo. Common pairings:
| Song key | Capo / shapes |
|---|---|
| A | Capo 2, G shapes |
| B | Capo 4, G shapes · capo 2, A shapes |
| Bb | Capo 3, G shapes · capo 1, A shapes |
| C | Open C · capo 5, G shapes |
| D | Open D · capo 2, C shapes |
| E | Open E · capo 4, C shapes |
| F | Capo 1, E shapes · capo 3, D shapes |

## The G family (acoustic staple)
G 3 2 0 0 3 3 · Cadd9 x 3 2 0 3 3 · Dsus4 x x 0 2 3 3 · Em7 0 2 2 0 3 3
The ring and pinky fingers stay on fret 3 of strings 2 and 1 for all four chords, so only the index and middle fingers move. This makes changes easier and keeps a shimmering drone ringing across every chord.

## Electric parts toolkit

### Dotted-eighth delay
- Delay time (ms) = 60000 ÷ BPM × 0.75. At 120 BPM that's 375 ms; at 72 BPM it's 625 ms.
- Quarter-note delay (ms) = 60000 ÷ BPM.
- Typical starting settings: 2–4 repeats (feedback around 25–35%), with the mix just under the dry signal.
- Technique: pick straight eighth notes, and the delay fills in the sixteenths. Pick evenly and keep the rhythm in your strumming hand.

### Root pulse
Eighth notes on the chord root, played high on string 4 or 3, light palm mute optional, with the dotted-eighth delay on. Change the root exactly on the chord change. This is the go-to part for pre-choruses and early bridges.

### Triads (top three strings)
Shapes are shown for strings 3-2-1, using G as the example. Transpose by sliding the shape.
| Inversion | Major (G) | Minor (Gm) |
|---|---|---|
| Root position (1-3-5) | 12 12 10 | 12 11 10 |
| 1st inversion (3-5-1) | 4 3 3 | 3 3 3 |
| 2nd inversion (5-1-3) | 7 8 7 | 7 8 6 |
For strings 4-3-2 in G: root position 5 4 3, 1st inversion 9 7 8, 2nd inversion 12 12 12 (open strings 0 0 0 also give a G triad).
Choose the inversion that sits closest to the previous chord, so the voicing moves as little as possible.

### Octaves
Mute the string in between the two notes with the underside of the finger fretting the lower note.
| Lower note on | Upper note | Fret offset |
|---|---|---|
| String 6 | String 4 | +2 |
| String 5 | String 3 | +2 |
| String 4 | String 2 | +3 |
| String 3 | String 1 | +3 |

### Double stops
- **Strings 3 & 2:** same fret = major 3rd; string 2 one fret lower = minor 3rd.
- **Strings 2 & 1:** string 1 one fret lower = major 3rd; two frets lower = minor 3rd.
- **Strings 3 & 1** (skip string 2): same fret = major 6th; string 1 one fret lower = minor 6th.
Slide through the scale's 6ths or 3rds under the melody. It's a classic worship and country sound.

### Drone voicings
**Key of E:** E 0 2 2 1 0 0 · Asus2 x 0 2 4 0 0 · Bsus4 x 2 4 4 0 0 · C#m7 x 4 6 4 0 0. Open B and E ring through every chord.
**Key of D:** D x x 0 2 3 2 · Dsus2 x x 0 2 3 0 · A7sus4 x 0 2 0 3 0 · G/B x 2 0 0 3 3 · Em7 0 2 2 0 3 3.

### Swells & pads
Roll the guitar's volume knob or a volume pedal from 0 up to full *after* picking each note or chord. Add a long reverb (and an optional shimmer or octave effect) to get a pad. Use pads on intros, verses, and tags.

### Tone guidelines (gear-agnostic)
| Section | Gain | Time-based effects |
|---|---|---|
| Verse / pads | Clean | Long reverb, swells |
| Pre-chorus | Clean to edge of breakup | Dotted-eighth delay |
| Chorus | Light overdrive | Delay + medium reverb |
| Bridge peak / final chorus | Stacked drive (medium) | Delay + reverb, sometimes modulation |
Roll back some low end: the bass player and the acoustic already cover that range.

## Song material & licensing
Hymns and traditional songs published in 1930 or earlier are public domain and can ship inside the app (see doc 00). Modern worship songs are copyrighted and licensed through CCLI/SongSelect, so the app should **link out** to them rather than include charts. The app can teach the *patterns* those songs use, like I–V–vi–IV with a dotted-eighth pulse, using original practice tracks.
